## My Class Notes: The Nervous System

The nervous system helps the brain and body communicate. In my notes, we divided it into two main parts:

- **Central nervous system:** The brain and spinal cord.
- **Peripheral nervous system:** The nerves outside the brain and spinal cord.

## My Class Notes: Brain Cells

Two important types of cells we discussed are **neurons** and **glial cells**.

- **Neurons:** Cells that send and receive signals.
- **Glial cells:** Cells that help support and protect neurons.
- **Dendrites:** Carry information toward the neuron's cell body.
- **Axon:** Carries signals away from the cell body.
- **Synapse:** The connection point where a neuron communicates with another cell.
- **Neurotransmitters:** Chemicals that help carry signals across a synapse.

### An Example From My Notes

If someone smells food cooking, the nervous system can help them notice the smell, start salivating, and move toward the kitchen. I thought this was a good example of how the brain and body work together.

## My Class Notes: Parts of the Brain

We learned about three broad sections of the brain:

1. **Hindbrain:** Toward the back and lower part of the brain.
2. **Midbrain:** A section that helps connect brain regions and process signals.
3. **Forebrain:** Includes areas involved in thinking, memory, and other complex functions.

We also went over four major brain structures:

| Brain structure | What it helps with |
|---|---|
| **Brainstem** | Breathing, heart rate, swallowing, and other basic functions. |
| **Cerebellum** | Movement, balance, and coordination. |
| **Diencephalon** | Important connections in sensory processing and regulation. |
| **Cerebrum** | Thinking, memory, and other complex activity. |

### More Notes on the Brainstem

My notes say that the brainstem helps regulate **breathing, heart rate, and blood vessel diameter**. It is also involved in things like **swallowing, coughing, and sneezing**.

## How This Connects to Sleep

Learning these parts of the brain gives me a foundation for understanding sleep. Different brain areas play roles in arousal, sensory processing, memory, and sleep, so it helps to know what they do first.
