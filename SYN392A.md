## About This Class

Hi, my name is **Michael Cruz**, and this page contains some of the notes I've taken in The Nuclear Age. We have been looking at how scientists learned about atoms and radioactivity, and how these discoveries later connected to warfare and the development of the atomic bomb.

## My Class Notes: Early Atomic Theory

- **Democritus:** Came up with the idea that matter is made of tiny particles called atoms.
- **John Dalton:** Proposed that elements are made of atoms and that elements combine in simple whole-number ratios to form compounds.
- **Dmitri Mendeleev:** Organized the periodic table based on patterns in the elements.
- **J. J. Thomson:** Discovered the electron, showing that atoms contain smaller particles.
- **Ernest Rutherford:** Used the gold foil experiment to show that an atom has a small, dense nucleus.
- **Niels Bohr:** Proposed an atomic model where electrons occupy specific energy levels.

## My Class Notes: Radioactivity and Scientific Discoveries

- **Henri Becquerel:** Discovered that uranium gives off radiation.
- **Wilhelm Röntgen:** Discovered X-rays.
- **Pierre and Marie Curie:** Studied radioactive elements. Marie Curie spent years isolating radium compounds.
- **James Chadwick:** Discovered the neutron, an electrically neutral particle in the atom.
- **Cyclotron:** A type of particle accelerator used for studying atomic particles.
- **Solvay Conference of 1927:** An important meeting of scientists working on new ideas in physics.

## My Class Notes: World War I and Science

We also talked about how science and war affected each other.

- **Fritz Haber** was connected to the development of chemical warfare in World War I.
- **Otto Hahn** worked in chemical warfare research during the war.
- **Lise Meitner** volunteered as an X-ray nurse during World War I.
- After the war, national borders changed, new political movements grew, and many countries remained upset about the peace agreements.
- **H. G. Wells** was a science fiction writer whose work connected science and possible futures.

## My Class Notes: The Manhattan Project

Later notes focused on the people and places behind the development of the atomic bomb during World War II.

| Person or place | What I wrote about |
|---|---|
| **General Leslie Groves** | Oversaw the Manhattan Project and focused on military organization and schedules. |
| **J. Robert Oppenheimer** | Led scientific work at Los Alamos and had to balance scientific needs with military rules. |
| **Oak Ridge, Tennessee** | Important for producing enriched uranium for the bomb project. |
| **Hanford, Washington** | Used to produce plutonium for the bomb project. |
| **Los Alamos, New Mexico** | A major site for bomb design and research. |
| **Argonne, Illinois** | Connected to nuclear research and the Chicago-based work that helped develop the project. |

My notes also mention different groups at Los Alamos, including theoretical physics, experimental work, chemistry and metallurgy, ordnance engineering, and work related to the bomb's design.

### Other Details From My Notes

- On **December 2, 1942**, scientists at the University of Chicago achieved the first controlled, self-sustaining nuclear chain reaction.
- The **Nuclear Energy** sculpture was unveiled 25 years later, on December 2, 1967, to mark that moment.
- **Richard Feynman** worked on computing problems at Los Alamos, including work involving IBM machines.
- **Fat Man** was an implosion-type atomic bomb that used explosive lenses.
- My notes also mention B-29 bombers and aircraft production in Wichita, Kansas.

## What I've Learned

One thing that stands out to me is how discoveries about atoms slowly led to technology that changed the world. We started with ideas about matter and moved into the history of World War I, World War II, and the Manhattan Project.
